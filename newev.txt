Like several French naval emblems, Émile Bertin had two distinct designs. The original badge was commissioned during the 1930s and featured a Japanese torii gate, likely a nod to the time the ship’s namesake Louis-Émile Bertin spent in Japan in the late 1880s. Given the conflict with Japan during the Second World War however, the design was understandably changed. This later design was created in the United States in 1943 when the cruiser was being refitted in Philadelphia. The panther on the emblem symbolised the cruiser’s impressive speed, while the tricolor silhouette of France was likely included as the cruiser was operating away from home while attached to the Free French forces. StuG.Brig 232 Emblem The emblem of Sturmgeschütz-Brigade 232 (232nd Assault Gun Brigade), Wehrmacht. The brigade began formation on November 10th 1942 at Jüterbog, and was ready by December. The initial plan was to send the unit to North Africa, but the worsening situation on the Eastern Front shifted the focus. It had a wide service life overall, initially attempting to support the 6th Army at Stalingrad before taking part in the various defensive battles that followed. It primarily fielded the StuH 42, StuG III F and StuG III G, holding around 30 operational vehicles by 1945. The emblem of the brigade was likely created sometime during 1944 as a stencil, depicting a StuG and an arm wielding a spiked flail; a black variant was also occasionally used.
