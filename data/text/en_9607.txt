Vehicle History (open spoiler): ▼
<> WZ-141-1 (China)
<>The WZ-141 prototype was developed in China in an attempt to create a light, air-droppable tank destroyer similar to the American M50 Ontos and Japanese Type 60. The vehicle’s tracked platform has bulletproof armor, and its main armament consists of two 105 mm recoilless guns. Development of the vehicle began in 1976, with the WZ-141-1 test model ready the following year. Despite successful testing, the WZ-141 was never accepted into production. In the game, the WZ-141-1 will be a mobile, compact and maneuverable tank destroyer with powerful weapons, allowing it to hide and ambush enemies.

<> A Premium Bomber for France at Rank III
Vehicle History (open spoiler): ▼
<> SB-25J (France)
<>American B-25 Mitchell bombers were not only used by the United States, but were actively supplied to Allied nations during the Second World War. A notable foreign operator of this plane was the Netherlands, who used the B-25 primarily in the Pacific theater. The SB-25J variant features eight large-caliber machine guns in the nose and four on the sides of the cockpit, giving this plane a combined total of 12 guns. The SB-25J also has excellent defensive armament and a good bomb load. In the game, this versatile medium bomber can operate effectively in both Air and Ground battles.

What is the Warbond Shop? (open spoiler): ▼
<>The Warbond shop is connected with and runs in tandem with the Battle Pass. Items in the Warbond shop can be purchased with a currency called Warbonds that are earned in the Battle Pass. There are a total of 6 shop levels and each one can be unlocked by reaching specific levels in the Battle Pass. In addition to purchasing with Warbonds, each item from shop level 3 requires a certain number of medals to purchase. Medals can be obtained through completing Special Tasks.
<>The Warbond shop can be accessed by clicking on Shop > Warbond Shop from the hangar or from the Battle Pass window by clicking “Shop”

<> Aigle (France)
<>Aigle features five main turrets with a single 138.6 mm gun in each, capable of firing HE or SAPBC. This Destroyer also has six torpedoes, an array of machine guns, and a 71 km/h top speed, making Aigle a good all-round Destroyer, capable of taking out a range of enemies.
<> Z47 (Germany)
<>The Type C Z47 was developed due to the shortcomings of the Type A and B. Featuring three main turrets with two 12.8 cm guns in each, the Z47 packs a punch against other enemy Destroyers. This ship also features twelve torpedoes and several 2 cm and 3.7 cm anti-aircraft guns.
<> Mörkö-Morane (Sweden)
<>The Mörkö-Morane is a Finnish variant of the French M.S.406, delivered in 1940. This variant features a single 12.7 mm machine gun or 20 mm cannon in the nose (swappable via modifications), one 7.5 mm machine gun on each wing, a good top speed of over 500 km/h and an excellent turn time.
<> Mustang Mk IA (Great Britain)
<>The Mustang Mk IA is a British variant of the classic American P-51. It features several unique camouflages, four 20 mm Hispano cannons that can deal heavy damage to enemy aircraft and great flight performance. It lacks suspended weaponry options, but the AP rounds fired by its cannons can tear through the top of enemy tanks with ease.
<> Grant I (USA)
<>The Grant I was derived from Britain needing a tank, they opted for the M3 Medium and modified it to their needs. This tank features two cannons; a fast-firing 37 mm gun in a conventional turret — which features significantly more armor than the Lee variant, along with a 75 mm at the side of the hull, firing the same powerful APHE shell as the early Shermans.
<> T-34-57 (1943) (USSR)
<>The T-34-57 (1943) was a late, experimental variant of the T-34, featuring the 57 mm ZIS-4M cannon in a modified hexagonal turret. It combines workable armor with effective mobility and a powerful cannon, with both high penetration and a very fast reload.
