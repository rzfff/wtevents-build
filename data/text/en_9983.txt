Vehicle History ▼
<>The B-52H modification was the last production version of the famous Boeing B-52 Stratofortress strategic bomber to date and the only one remaining in service with the US Air Force to this day.
<>Development began in January 1959, based on the B-52G version. The main difference were the new engines: the J57 turbojet engines were replaced by more fuel-efficient and powerful TF33-P-1 turbofan engines.
<>The B-52H’s maiden flight took place in March 1961, and the aircraft entered service with the US Air Force’s Strategic Air Command that same month. A total of 102 aircraft of this modification were built between 1960 and 1962. The last production aircraft was delivered in June 1962.
<>The B-52H, which has undergone multiple, extensive modernization programs, remains the backbone of US strategic aviation, demonstrating unprecedented longevity in aviation history. They are expected to remain combat-ready until the 2050s, almost a century after the first examples were built!

Vehicle History ▼
<>Development of the Tu-95 strategic bomber began in 1951 amidst the intense confrontation between the USSR and the United States. Designers were tasked with creating an intercontinental nuclear weapons carrier capable of reaching potential enemy territory.
<>The first prototype was built by 1952 and submitted for testing in Zhukovsky, which highlighted the need to improve the aircraft’s performance. In 1956, the decision was made to thoroughly modernize it with more powerful engines.
<>An updated version, designated the Tu-95M, was put into serial production and subsequently upgraded several times, remaining in service until the 1980s.

<> Anti-radiation missiles
<>There’s a new weapon in the game: anti-radiation missiles (ARM)! These are designed to destroy radio sources, primarily the radars of medium and long-range anti-aircraft systems. During the event, these missiles will not have the guidance error inherent in real-life missiles, a proximity sensor, or frequency limitations so that they can hit anti-aircraft systems more frequently.
<>But don’t think these missiles will destroy anti-aircraft guns in a jiffy. The sites of medium- and long-range anti-aircraft systems are covered with other anti-aircraft systems for self-defense, moreover, all SAMs and SPAAs in the event can locate and attack not just the aircraft, but munitions as well, so to successfully penetrate air defenses, you’ll have to work together with other pilots to overload the anti-aircraft defenses in a given area.
<>The following missiles are presented:
<> The AGM-88A HARM is an American anti-radiation missile with a maximum launch range of up to 150 km.
<> The Kh-58U is a Soviet anti-radiation missile with a maximum launch range of up to 120 km.
<> The Kh-25MPU is a Soviet anti-radiation missile with a maximum launch range of up to 40 km.

The MiG-23MLD can carry the RN-28 bomb, a 5-kiloton thermonuclear warhead. The Su-24M carries the 30-kiloton RN-40. And the Tu-95M "Bear" carries the RDS-37, a 1.6-megaton bomb.
<>In the event, the B-52H and Tu-95M will be protected by very powerful ECM systems, setting up noise and deception jamming, which will make it impossible to attack them with any radar-homing missiles and will make it harder to find them with the radar.
<> Fight on the Tropical Coast
<>Welcome to the Tropical Coast, a special map created for this massive battle. It was inspired by the landscape of Central America, where snow-capped mountain ranges give way to dense tropical jungle, with valleys and gorges dotted with small villages in between. The total area of ​​the new location is comparable to countries like Sri Lanka, Croatia, or Lithuania!
<> The frontline
<>The front is maintained by supply lines from the rear. The central element is the tactical assembly area with fuel, ammunition, and armored vehicle depots. This is where fuel and ammunition supply convoys from rear depots proceed, and from there, armored vehicle convoys are formed heading toward the front: to resupply strongpoints and for the assault.
<>To advance, you must attack depots, troop deployment sites, and enemy columns. However, you will be confronted by anti-aircraft guns of various types: from short and medium-range systems like the Shilka, Gepard, Strela, Osa, FlaRakPz I, Buk, and Hawk, to truly long-range Patriot and S-300P air defense systems.
<>However, you don’t have to do all the work yourself. Supporting and covering allied ground forces can greatly help hold or successfully attack a section of the front. And we’re not just talking about the usual columns of tanks and cannon artillery! The teams have mobile M270 and Smerch MLRS crews, as well as Lance and Tochka tactical missile systems with cluster warhead missiles, which can reach critical areas from a long range. By taking out the enemy’s long-range artillery systems, you can drastically lower the losses of allied armored vehicles which will significantly aid them in the attack on the enemy positions.
<> Escalation levels
<>As the battle progresses, both sides suffer losses, increasing the escalation level. There are three levels in total, and as each level is reached, increasingly powerful nuclear weapons become available.
<>At the very beginning tactical nuclear weapons are unavailable and the battle is fought with conventional means. However, starting from the first escalation level the conflict reaches the nuclear stage, where for spawn points access is granted to 5 kt bombs, at level two, to 30 kt bombs. At level three, the maximum, access is granted to the B-52H and Tu-95M strategic bombers, with bomb yields of 1.45 Mt and 1.6 Mt respectively.
<> Airfields
<>Airfields will become milestones in the advance to victory. Penetrate enemy defenses, destroy smaller enemy airfields and try to reach the main objective — a huge airbase deep within the defensive lines. Its destruction will end the standoff and ensure immediate victory in the mission! Effectively destroying these strategic targets will require the most powerful weapons, so suppress resistance on the ground and deliver strategic nuclear munitions to them!
